Sources

Everything used to recreate the glaciers of the Last Glacial Maximum, and what each is used for

Field evidence of ice extent

Where the ice reached. The climate is fitted to it, every result is scored against it, and the map can draw it as "mapped extent".

GLACIMONTIS: mountain paleoglaciers worldwide

Lima et al. 2026, Scientific Data. Data: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15600659

The outlines of mountain glaciers. Around Mount Rainier it draws only the valley tongues, so the comparison there is limited to ground below 1,100 m.

Northern Hemisphere ice sheets at the LGM

Batchelor et al. 2019, "The configuration of Northern Hemisphere ice sheets through the Quaternary", Nature Communications 10, 3713. Data: osf.io/7jen3

The best-estimate outline of the ice sheets, including the lobes that entered northern Idaho. Not used around Rainier, where it covers the Cascades whole.

Ice-age climate

How much colder and drier it was: the change from pre-industrial to the LGM, added to today's climate.

PMIP4 / CMIP6 climate-model simulations

The lgm and piControl runs of AWI-ESM-1-1-LR, INM-CM4-8, MIROC-ES2L and MPI-ESM1-2-LR, from the Earth System Grid Federation (DKRZ node). Overview: Kageyama et al. 2021, "The PMIP4 Last Glacial Maximum experiments", Climate of the Past 17, 1065-1089.

The mean of the four models. They resolve the ice sheets but not single ranges, which is why a correction is fitted to the field evidence.

CHELSA-TraCE21k (tested, not used)

Karger et al. 2023, Climate of the Past.

A 1 km downscaled alternative. Its cooling over central Idaho was no closer to what the glaciers imply.

Present-day climate

Monthly temperature and precipitation today, the baseline the ice-age change is added to.

PRISM 1991-2020 normals, 800 m

PRISM Climate Group, Oregon State University, prism.oregonstate.edu

Used for every mountain region: it resolves how much more snow falls on the ranges than in the valleys.

WorldClim 2.1

Fick & Hijmans 2017, International Journal of Climatology 37, 4302-4315.

Used outside the United States: at 4.6 km for the Pyrenees, and at 18 km for the coarse North America run that supplies the ice sheets.

Terrain

The ground the ice grows on.

Terrain Tiles

Mapzen, hosted as AWS Open Data: registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles

Today's terrain (10-30 m sources in the US), for the model's mountain regions and for the 3D view.

ETOPO 2022 bedrock

NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information, doi.org/10.25921/fd45-gt74

Bedrock with today's ice sheets removed, for the North America run.

Mount Rainier's lost summit

Why Rainier stands taller here than it does today.

The Osceola collapse

Vallance & Scott 1997, "The Osceola Mudflow from Mount Rainier", GSA Bulletin 109, 143-163.

About 5,600 years ago the summit and north-east flank collapsed. The height before that, about 4,900 m (16,000 ft), is the commonly quoted USGS estimate. The cone put back here is a plain cone of about that height and volume, not a geological reconstruction.

Checks against field studies

Not inputs: studies the results were compared with afterwards.

The Wind River ice cap

Birkel et al. 2012, "Climate inferences from a glaciological reconstruction of the late Pleistocene Wind River Ice Cap", Arctic, Antarctic, and Alpine Research 44, 265-276.

Describes a plateau ice cap with lobes reaching the basin floors, the shape the model also produces.

Glaciation of the Sawtooth Valley

Thackray et al. 2004, Geology 32, 225-228; USGS Open-File Report 2004-1222.

Glacier lobes from the Sawtooths reached into Stanley Basin and built the moraines along the range front.

Methods

The physics in the model.

Ice flow: the shallow-ice approximation

Hutter 1983, Theoretical Glaciology.

Melt: positive degree-days

Calov & Greve 2005, Journal of Glaciology 51, 173-175.

Melt in proportion to the warmth above freezing, allowing for day-to-day temperature swings.
